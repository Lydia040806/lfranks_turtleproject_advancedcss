Common Types of Turtles

Sea Turtles

There are seven species of sea turtles, and they live in oceans all around the world. The green sea turtle is a large marine turtle found in tropical and subtropical waters. Adults mainly eat seagrass and algae, which gives their fat a greenish color. The leatherback is the largest living turtle and can weigh over 900 kilograms. Instead of a hard shell, it has a flexible, leathery back with ridges along its length. Leatherbacks dive deeper than most turtles and feed mostly on jellyfish. Loggerheads are named for their large heads and have powerful jaws for crushing shellfish.

Not all turtles live in the sea, and many species are found in lakes, ponds, and forests. Painted turtles are common in freshwater ponds and are easy to spot by the bright red and yellow markings on their bodies. Box turtles live on land and have a hinged shell that lets them close up completely when threatened. Snapping turtles are known for their strong bite and can live in muddy rivers and swamps. Tortoises are a separate group that live only on land and eat mostly grasses and leaves. The giant tortoises of the Galapagos Islands and Aldabra can live well over a century. Because each species has adapted to its own habitat, turtles show an amazing variety of shapes, sizes, and behaviors.

Did You Know?

The temperature of the sand where eggs are laid decides whether hatchlings are male or female.
