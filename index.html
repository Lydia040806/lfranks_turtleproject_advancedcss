An Introduction to These Ancient Reptiles

Where They Live

Turtles are reptiles with a hard shell that protects their bodies. They have lived on Earth for over 200 million years, which means they shared the planet with the dinosaurs. The shell is made of bone covered by plates called scutes, and it is actually part of the turtle's skeleton. Turtles can be found in oceans, rivers, ponds, and on land across almost every continent except Antarctica. Sea turtles spend nearly their whole lives in the water and only come ashore to nest. Freshwater turtles often bask on logs and rocks to warm their bodies in the sun. Tortoises are land dwellers with sturdy, dome-shaped shells and thick, stumpy legs.

Turtles begin life as eggs that the mother buries in a nest dug into sand or soil. After several weeks or months, the hatchlings break out and must find their way to the water on their own. Many young turtles are eaten by birds, crabs, and fish before they reach safety. Those that survive can grow slowly for decades, and some species live for more than 100 years. Turtles have no teeth, so they use sharp beaks to bite and chew their food. Diets vary widely, with some turtles eating plants, others eating insects and fish, and many eating both. Today many species are threatened by pollution, habitat loss, and fishing nets, so conservation efforts are very important.
