Protecting Turtles for the Future

Threats They Face

Many turtle species around the world are now threatened or endangered. Hawksbill turtles were hunted for decades for their beautiful shells, which nearly drove them to extinction. Plastic bags in the ocean look like jellyfish, and turtles that swallow them can become very sick. Fishing nets and lines also trap turtles, which cannot reach the surface to breathe. Beaches where turtles nest are often lost to buildings, and bright lights can confuse hatchlings. Rising temperatures are making nests warmer, which changes the balance of male and female hatchlings. Because turtles take many years to mature, populations recover very slowly once they decline.

People around the globe are working hard to give turtles a better chance. Volunteers patrol beaches at night to protect nests from predators and poachers. Some groups move threatened nests to safe hatcheries and release the hatchlings once they are ready. Special escape hatches on fishing nets, called turtle excluder devices, let trapped turtles swim free. Many countries have passed laws that make it illegal to harm turtles or sell their shells. Cleaning up beaches and reducing single-use plastic helps keep the ocean safe for them. Schools and nature centers teach children why these animals matter to healthy seas and rivers. Everyone can help by recycling, picking up litter, and respecting wildlife on the beach.

How You Can Help

  • Reduce your use of single-use plastics.
  • Join a local beach or river cleanup.
  • Keep lights off near nesting beaches at night.
  • Never take turtle products home as souvenirs.
